# The personal playbook

Built only from the resume (`work/resume.txt`) and the job description (`work/job.md`). It is the part of the guide that makes it personal, and the part where invention does the most damage.

## The honesty rule

- Every fact about the candidate must appear in the resume, or be something the candidate told you in this conversation.
- Quote numbers exactly as the resume gives them. Do not round up, do not merge two roles into one, do not stretch a title.
- A requirement with no evidence is a **gap**. Write "No direct evidence in the resume" and give an honest reframe. Never fill the gap with a plausible guess.
- If something is ambiguous ("led a team": how many people? direct reports?), ask the user or phrase it at the level the resume supports.

Why: interviewers check. One inflated claim discovered in the room makes every other true claim suspect.

## 1. How to position

3 to 6 bullets. Each one: a bold claim the candidate can own, then the resume evidence behind it, then why it matters for this role. Lead with the strongest overlap between the candidate's history and the company's problem.

## 2. Resume evidence for each requirement

One row per requirement from the job description (must-haves first, then nice-to-haves):

| Requirement | Evidence from the resume | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| copied or summarised from the JD | the specific role, project, number | `strong`, `partial` or `gap` |

- `strong`: direct, recent, quantified evidence.
- `partial`: related or smaller-scale evidence; say what is missing.
- `gap`: no evidence. Say so.

## 3. Likely objections and honest reframes

Two to four objections an interviewer could raise from the gaps and partials. For each:
1. Acknowledge what is true.
2. Offer the closest real evidence.
3. Close with a plan or a question that shows judgement.

Never deny a gap the resume makes obvious.

## 4. Three STAR stories

Pick three stories from the resume that together cover: why this company (domain fit), a measurable result, and working with others under pressure. Each has Situation, Task, Action, Result (with the number from the resume) and "use it for" listing the interview questions it answers. Keep each part to one or two sentences so the candidate can say the whole story in under two minutes.

## 5. Questions to ask the interviewers

Five or six. Good questions reveal how the role is measured, what is hard right now, and how decisions are made. Each should change how the candidate would do the job. Avoid questions answered on the website.

## 6. Do and don't

Four or five of each, specific to this company and this candidate: the traps not to repeat, the numbers to label, the controversy not to take sides on, the gap to name first.

## 7. Numbers cheat sheet

The few numbers worth memorising: company scale, pricing, funding, key dates, and the candidate's own numbers. Each line short, each number already verified or labelled in the overview.

## 8. 30-60-90 day skeleton

Three phases (learn and measure, first wins, scale), three items each, grounded in what the company said it needs (job post, funding announcement, roadmap). Offer it in the interview before being asked.

## When the user asked to keep the playbook general

Replace employer names and exact figures with descriptions ("a payments company", "cut onboarding time by more than 90 percent") and keep the same structure.
